Most kitchen gadgets are solutions looking for a problem. These are the
opposite: things I reach for almost every day, that cost very little and
replace something annoying about cooking.

## Start under ₹500

If you cook even three times a week, chopping onions and garlic is where the
time goes. A pull-cord **vegetable chopper** turns that five-minute job into
about thirty seconds, and because everything lands in its own container, the
counter stays clean.

My rule for cheap kitchen tools: if it has more than one moving part, skip it.
The pull-cord design is simple enough that it keeps working.

## The upgrade that is worth stretching for

Silicone tools cost a little more than the plastic ones, but they last far
longer and they do not scratch a non-stick pan. If your current spatula has a
melted edge or a gap where water collects, replacing it is a genuine
quality-of-life improvement rather than a gadget purchase.

## How I choose what goes on this list

1. I have to have used it for at least a month.
2. It has to replace something I was already doing — not create a new chore.
3. It has to be easy to clean. Anything that takes longer to wash than to use
   does not make the list.

> A good kitchen tool disappears into your routine. If you have to remember to
> use it, it is not a good tool.
